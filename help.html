It won't open, or it's stuck loading

Try these in order. Stop as soon as it opens.

  1. Wait 15 seconds. If it is still loading, a button appears: Start in the plain view. Tap it. The globe opens with fewer layers, which is lighter for a slow phone or a weak signal.
  2. Still stuck after 30 seconds? Tap Reset the app on this device. This throws away the saved copy of the app and loads it fresh. Your saved maps, news, books and plan stay.
  3. Close it fully. Swipe up from the bottom edge and hold until your open apps appear, then push the terminal (or Safari) up and off the screen. Open it again.Close it fully. Tap the square button at the bottom, or swipe up from the bottom and hold, then swipe the terminal (or Chrome) away. Open it again.Close the tab or the window, then open the terminal again.
  4. Open this page on its own and tap the button below. It does the same as step 2, from here, even when the globe can't start. The address of this page is .
  5. If none of that works, remove the site's data with the steps in Free up space or remove it completely, then open the terminal again with signal.

Put it on your Home Screen

It then opens full screen from its own icon, like any other app, and your phone keeps its saved copy longer.

You are using the Home Screen copy right now. Nothing to do here.

  1. Open the terminal in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button: a square with an arrow pointing up. On newer iPhones, tap the three dots at the bottom right first, then Share.
  3. Scroll down the list and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. If you see Open as Web App, leave it switched on. Tap Add.
  5. From now on, open it from the new icon on your Home Screen.

In Chrome on iPhone it works the same way: tap the Share button at the top right of the address bar, then Add to Home Screen.

Good to know: the Home Screen copy keeps its own saved data, apart from Safari. Maps and books you saved in Safari are not in it, so save them again from the Home Screen copy.

  1. Open the terminal in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots at the top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen. On some phones it is called Install app.
  4. Tap Install (or Add), and if asked, tap Add once more to place the icon.
  5. From now on, open it from the new icon.
  1. Open the terminal in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Look at the right end of the address bar for a small screen with a down arrow, and click it. If it isn't there, open the browser menu (three dots) and choose Install, or in Edge Apps then Install this site as an app.
  3. Click Install. It opens in its own window and gets an icon like other programs.

Safari on a Mac: choose File, then Add to Dock. Firefox can't install sites, but everything else on this page still works in it.

Using it with no signal

The terminal saves itself and the latest news on your device while you have signal. With no signal it opens from that saved copy and shows how old everything is.

  1. With signal, open the terminal and wait until the globe and the news have loaded. That saves the app and today's picture.
  2. Open the Field Kit once. At the top, Ready for no signal lists what is still missing on this device.
  3. In the Field Kit, open Here and save the street maps of your area. Get any encyclopedias you want in Library.
  4. Open it about once a week with signal. Each visit saves the newest news over the old.

Works with no signal

  • The globe, with the whole Earth and the places you already looked at
  • The last saved news, levels and layers, each with its time
  • Street maps of areas you saved
  • The Field Kit: survival guide, tools, country files, your plan and go-bag
  • Encyclopedias you downloaded
  • Passing the news to another phone: screen to camera, same Wi-Fi, a file, or a long-range radio

Needs signal

  • Anything new: news, levels, alerts
  • Live planes, ships, cameras, TV and radio
  • Weather radar and new forecasts
  • Close-up 3D cities and street view
  • Maps of places you didn't save
  • Downloading books and maps

When a phone deletes the saved copy

An iPhone can delete what a website saved in two cases:

  • You use Safari for about a week without opening the terminal. The copy on your Home Screen doesn't count this way, which is one more reason to add it there.
  • The iPhone runs very low on storage and frees space on its own.

Android phones keep it unless the phone runs very low on storage, or you clear Chrome's data. Then the phone may free space by deleting what websites saved.

Computers keep it unless the disk is nearly full, or you clear the browser's history with "cookies and site data" ticked.

  1. Ask the device to keep it: tap Keep it on this device below. Not every phone says yes; it is more likely once it is on your Home Screen and you use it often.
  2. If it was deleted anyway, open the terminal once with signal. It saves itself and the news again on its own. Saved street maps and encyclopedias need saving again.

How much space it uses

These numbers are measured on this device, right now.

Measuring

    Rough sizes before you download: one area of street maps is usually 5 to 30 MB. Encyclopedias go from about 5 MB (the 100 most read Wikipedia articles) to about 800 MB for WikiMed, the medical encyclopedia, and about 1 GB for Simple English Wikipedia. Each one shows its size in Library before you get it. The offline assistant on a USB stick uses no phone storage at all; it needs about 3.5 GB on the stick.

    This also removes your plan, contacts, go-bag list, saved news history and settings. It can't be undone.

    You can also do it from the device's own settings. That works even when the terminal won't open.

    In Safari

    1. Open the Settings app.
    2. Tap Apps, then Safari. On iOS 17 or older there is no Apps step: scroll down and tap Safari.
    3. Scroll to the bottom and tap Advanced, then Website Data.
    4. Wait for the list to load, then type in the search box at the top.
    5. Swipe left on it and tap Delete.

    The Home Screen copy

    1. Touch and hold the terminal's icon on your Home Screen.
    2. Tap Remove App (or Delete Bookmark), then Delete.
    3. That removes the icon and everything the Home Screen copy saved. Safari's copy is separate; remove it with the steps above.

    To see how much space each website takes first: Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, then Safari, then Website Data.

    In Chrome

    1. Open Chrome and tap the three dots at the top right.
    2. Tap Settings, then Site settings.
    3. Tap All sites (on some phones it is called Data stored).
    4. Find in the list and tap it.
    5. Tap Delete data (on older Chrome: Clear & reset), then confirm.

    If you put it on your Home Screen, touch and hold its icon and choose Uninstall or Remove. The saved data lives in Chrome, so also do the steps above. In other browsers, look in their settings for Site settings or Website data.

    Chrome or Edge

    1. Open the terminal.
    2. Click the small icon at the left end of the address bar, then Site settings.
    3. Click Delete data and confirm.

    In Edge the menu item is called Permissions for this site, and the button is Clear data.

    Firefox

    1. Open the terminal and click the padlock at the left end of the address bar.
    2. Click Clear cookies and site data, then Remove.

    Safari on a Mac

    1. Choose Safari, then Settings, then Privacy.
    2. Click Manage Website Data and search for .
    3. Select it and click Remove.

    When it fixes itself on start

    The terminal watches its own start. If it can't finish, it fixes itself instead of leaving you on a loading screen.

    • If the last visit froze or the phone closed it, the next one opens in the plain view: the globe with fewer layers on. A note at the top says so.
    • If it stops on start because of something saved on this device, it puts your layer settings aside, loads a fresh copy of itself and opens the plain view. Your maps, news and plan are not touched.
    • If that fails too, it shows what went wrong and a Reset the app on this device button. It never reloads over and over.
    1. When you see the note, tap Restore my layers to get your layers and settings back.
    2. If it freezes again with your layers on, leave them off and turn on only the ones you need from Filters.
